# Health, airports, cycling and journey-time evidence for Ireland

Research observation: **4 October 2026**, Republic of Ireland scope. This is a source and design investigation; no router was installed, no provider key was created and no household journey-time result was calculated. Existing [national services research](ireland-national-services.md) and [transport/location research](ireland-transport-location.md) supply the school, childcare, hospital-location and NTA feed evidence; their downloads were not repeated here.

## Decision

The data can support a national map and modelled road journeys, but **current GP availability and hospital suitability require additional evidence**. A historical licensed GP snapshot is available; the current HSE website is not a verified licensed bulk feed. An airport fallback is openly reusable. Verified protected-cycle geometry is regional, not a national completeness claim. Treat the user's hospital-within-30-minutes and GP-within-one-hour-drive examples as actual journey criteria: **1,800 and 3,600 seconds**, respectively. Hospital mode and required service must be selected; a map radius cannot evaluate either requirement.

## GPs: useful historical fallback, current access unresolved

The [ArcGIS item metadata](https://www.arcgis.com/sharing/rest/content/items/01cb04a1fab34c72a746dc660622fe73?f=json) explicitly licences the dataset CC BY 4.0 and identifies Health Atlas data supplied in March 2020. The item's December 2025 modification does not establish updated GP observations: the [layer editing metadata](https://services1.arcgis.com/eNO7HHeQ3rUcBllm/arcgis/rest/services/GeneralPractitionersHSEIreland/FeatureServer/0?f=pjson) retains a 2020 data-edit timestamp.

Two independently fetched queries verified a [6,608-row count](https://services1.arcgis.com/eNO7HHeQ3rUcBllm/arcgis/rest/services/GeneralPractitionersHSEIreland/FeatureServer/0/query?where=1%3D1&returnCountOnly=true&f=json) and [one GeoJSON sample with server reprojection](https://services1.arcgis.com/eNO7HHeQ3rUcBllm/arcgis/rest/services/GeneralPractitionersHSEIreland/FeatureServer/0/query?where=1%3D1&outFields=*&outSR=4326&resultRecordCount=1&f=geojson). Fields include `ID`, `Service`, `ServiceName`, `GPSurname`, `GPFirstname`, address components, `Eircode`, telephone, `Opening_Hours`, `Website`, `lat`, `lon`. The sample Cremore Clinic geometry is `[-6.27651428353659, 53.3816643225904]`, while its **`lat=-6.2765` and `lon=53.3817` attribute labels are swapped**. Use reprojected geometry, not those attributes. Counts are records containing individual clinicians, not verified distinct practices; sample opening hours were null.

The [current HSE finder](https://www2.hse.ie/services/find-a-gp/) displayed 2,498 results during observation, including names, contact details, addresses and directions. This number is not comparable to the clinician-level historical count. No documented public bulk endpoint or explicit data-reuse licence was verified. It also does not establish that a practice accepts new patients, offers the required service or is open at a proposed arrival time. Preserve those as separate unknown fields; travel to a mapped practice is only a geographic access measure.

## Hospital location is not the required care destination

The [current HSE finder](https://www2.hse.ie/services/find-urgent-emergency-care/?kind=emergency-department) distinguishes emergency departments, injury units, GP out-of-hours, early-pregnancy assessment and maternity emergency services, and provides age/opening filters. Examples illustrate why nearest-point logic fails: [Beaumont ED](https://www2.hse.ie/services/find-urgent-emergency-care/beaumont-hospital-emergency-department/) lists age 16 and above, while [CHI Crumlin ED](https://www2.hse.ie/services/find-urgent-emergency-care/childrens-health-ireland-at-crumlin-emergency-department/) lists age 15 and younger. The [HSE ED explanation](https://www2.hse.ie/emergencies/the-emergency-department-ed/) also distinguishes ED care from injury units and GP out-of-hours.

The text extraction showed “Closed now” against ED examples. **This was not independently verified as clinical operating status** and must not become a live closure signal. Service type, eligible ages, opening schedule and accessible entrance need a validated source and freshness policy. Current finder reuse/API remains unverified. The historical licensed hospital layer is adequate for a clearly dated facility overlay, not current specialty/ED availability, clinical suitability, beds, waiting times or admissions.

## Airport destination and national cycling gaps

The [IAA](https://www.iaa.ie/commercial-aviation/aerodromes-6) points users to AIP aerodrome status. The [Dublin AIP page effective 1 October 2026](https://www.airnav.ie/AIRAC/2026-10-01-AIRAC/html/eAIP/EI-AD-2.EIDW-en-IE.html) was accessible and identifies Dublin International/EIDW. Its reference coordinate `532517N 0061612W` is the midpoint of runway 10R/28L. Route travellers to a public terminal access point or transit stop, not an aerodrome reference point that can snap to private airside roads. Official bulk open licensing was not verified.

The [OurAirports Irish export](https://ourairports.com/countries/IE/airports.csv), licensed public domain by its [publisher](https://ourairports.com/data/), is immediately usable as a fallback. Parsed type counts: 21 closed, 11 heliport, 4 large airport, 6 medium airport, 1 seaplane base and 94 small airport. Fields include `ident`, `type`, `name`, `latitude_deg`, `longitude_deg`, `municipality`, `scheduled_service`, ICAO/IATA identifiers, `home_link` and `last_updated`. In this country export, `scheduled_service` uses **0/1**, not yes/no. The six flagged scheduled locations were Dublin, Shannon, Cork, Kerry, Knock and Donegal; this does not verify a current route or timetable. Individual observation dates ranged from Donegal 2009 to Cork/Knock 2026. Refreshing the download does not refresh those observations.

The [DCC/NTA protected-cycle resource](https://data.smartdublin.ie/dataset/dublin-metropolitan-area-existing-protected-cycle-infrastructure-2025) describes segregated tracks/lanes, signed quietways, car-free links and urban greenways; it excludes ordinary painted lanes and bus lanes. Features can represent both sides of roads. The [download](https://data.smartdublin.ie/dataset/0bf91d3f-09aa-4700-a1f9-e2351e4de5f4/resource/9e1ab797-7249-4f01-b172-740637d8cff9/download/dublin-cycle-infrastructure-2025.json) parsed as 4,826 LineStrings, 2,815,459 bytes, declared **EPSG:3857**. Sample properties: `FID`, `cdo_1=SegregatedCycleLane`, `twoway`, `bollardpro` and several length fields; sample coordinates `[-700264.3873,7047539.5231]`. Transform to WGS84 before serving normal GeoJSON; validate length-field units rather than assuming them.

No nationally complete, current protected-cycle inventory was verified. [TII's National Cycle Network](https://www.tii.ie/en/news/tii-news/national-cycling-network/) describes a phased plan of 85 corridors and approximately 3,500 km, incorporating existing and future infrastructure. Do not score proposed corridors as built routes. OSM is a possible nationwide routing fallback with incomplete tags/coverage; a routable bicycle trip is not proof of continuous protected lanes or child-safe infrastructure.

## Routing choices and the evidence needed for minutes

**OSRM** is a practical candidate for a road-time matrix. Its [official versioned API](https://project-osrm.org/docs/v5.24.0/api/) returns directed Table durations in seconds and distances in metres; coordinates use longitude, latitude. Vehicle profiles are set during preprocessing: a URL label does not create a bicycle graph. `NoSegment`/`NoRoute`/null durations are unresolved results. **Disable `fallback_speed` and reject any `fallback_speed_cells`: those values are explicitly straight-line speed approximations.** They cannot satisfy the 30/60-minute requirements. An optional `data_version` can record underlying OSM time; save graph/profile versions as well.

**Valhalla** can support car, bicycle and pedestrian matrices and reachable-area maps. Its [matrix reference](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/matrix/api-reference/) provides source-to-target times and date/time options. Its [isochrone reference](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/isochrone/api-reference/) provides minute contours; `reverse=true` answers where a destination can be reached from, which matters with one-way roads. Contours are a screening visualization; run candidate routes/matrix checks for the actual threshold. Time-dependent request support does not supply Irish traffic data: without a validated traffic input, label results as **modelled driving time**, not current congestion. Neither engine was run here. Their demonstration services are not an approved application backend; select a hosted provider with verified terms or build a reproducible graph.

**OpenTripPlanner** is the relevant separate candidate for scheduled public transport. Its [build configuration](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/BuildConfiguration/) combines streets and GTFS/NeTEx service data. Its [planning API documentation](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/api/dev-2.x/graphql-gtfs/queries/plan) supports departure/arrival selection, mode, transfer and accessibility preferences; that documented `plan` query is now deprecated in favour of `planConnection`, so implementation must pin a supported release/API. A transit result must include street access, departure wait, boarding/transfer time and destination walk, not just vehicle time.

The [existing NTA investigation](ireland-transport-location.md) verified feed availability and a LUAS sample, not all national schedules. Import the full national archive; check each service's `calendar` and `calendar_dates`, feed coverage, agency timezone, holiday changes and after-midnight service times. The sampled LUAS agency timezone was `Europe/London`; preserve source timezone and convert user instants correctly. A scheduled trip is not a real-time promise. The NTA live service requires registration/key access, which remains unconfigured. Stops without active trips do not demonstrate usable transport.

## Suggested evaluation contract and remaining gates

Keep configurable weights separate from mandatory eligibility. Before ranking, identify a suitable service destination, a precise household origin and a valid journey observation. A provisional API result could carry `criterion`, `threshold_seconds`, `mode`, `target_id`, `service_type`, `eligibility_status`, `duration_seconds`, `depart_at`, `timezone`, `engine`, `profile_version`, `graph_date`, `schedule_date`, `traffic_basis`, `origin_precision`, `destination_precision`, `status` and `reason`. Use `pass`, `fail` and `unknown`; missing/stale/unsuitable targets or failed routing produce **unknown**, not a passing default or zero minutes. Keep accepting-patients/clinical-fit unknown distinct from a computed geographic travel result.

Hospital access needs selected service/age eligibility and selected mode before testing `duration_seconds <= 1800`. GP driving access uses a genuine car route and `duration_seconds <= 3600`, with availability shown separately. Define whether parking/entrance walking count in the threshold. Airport access should use the selected passenger airport/terminal, mode and departure/arrival time; airline check-in/security time is a different requirement. Car ownership, bicycle preferences and transit availability should change eligible modes rather than merely change POI distance weights.

Still unproven: current licensed national GP/service-capability imports; practice deduplication and accepting-patients status; destination entrances; full GTFS validation; road graph build; traffic assumptions; actual router responses; bicycle-network completeness and route suitability. Before claiming any household meets a time requirement, verify sample journeys across urban/rural areas, one-way approaches, disconnected roads, invalid service days and near-threshold cases. Reusable source availability is established for several layers; end-to-end journey compliance is not established by this research.
